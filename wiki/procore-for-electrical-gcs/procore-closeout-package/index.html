Building the closeout package as you go

Based on Procore's public help pages, November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: almost everything in a closeout package already passes through Procore during the job, so file it in a closeout folder as it arrives and the package is nearly done at substantial completion.

What goes in an electrical closeout package

DocumentWhere it comes from in Procore
As-built drawingsDrawings markups and revisions
Approved submittalsSubmittals tool
Test reports (megger, OTDR, ground resistance, functional tests)Inspections, Documents
O&M manuals and warrantiesSubmittals (closeout items) or Documents
Equipment list with serial numbersInspections or a register in Documents
Certificates of complianceSubmittals or Documents
Photos of buried workPhotos
Final RFI and change logsRFIs, Change Orders

See Closeout package checklist for what Caltrans expects.

Setting it up

  • Add closeout submittals to the register from day one (O&M manuals, warranties, as-builts), each with its spec reference.
  • Create a Closeout folder structure in Documents that mirrors the package.
  • Keep red-line as-builts on the drawings as work is installed, not from memory at the end.

Exporting

When the job ends, export the package: Procore's project archive tool (formerly Data Extracts) can export organized records and documents from tools like Submittals, Drawings, RFIs, Daily Log and Documents, with filters and attachments. See Keeping your records after the job.

PM notes

Make closeout documents a condition of a sub's retention release. They're easiest to get while the sub still wants to be paid.

Common mistakes

  • Collecting O&M manuals and warranties after the job ends.
  • As-builts drawn from memory.
  • Test reports kept in personal email.
