Meetings, correspondence and the schedule

Based on Procore's public help pages, November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: the formal record of a job is more than RFIs and submittals: meeting minutes, letters, notices and the schedule belong in the same place so you can find them when it matters.

Meetings

  • Agenda, attendees, minutes and action items with owners and due dates.
  • Recurring series for the weekly meeting with the RE and the internal coordination meeting.
  • Action items carried forward until closed.

Typical Caltrans meetings to record: the preconstruction meeting, weekly progress meetings, safety meetings, and meetings with utility companies.

Correspondence

Procore offers a Correspondence tool for formal communication types your company defines, for example:

  • letters to and from the RE;
  • notices of potential extra work, delay or differing site conditions;
  • transmittals.

Each type gets its own numbered log. Notices are where deadlines live: record the date sent and how it was sent. See Claims and disputes on public contracts.

Schedule

Upload or connect the baseline schedule and its updates so the team sees the current one. Caltrans contracts set schedule submission requirements in the special provisions; keep each submitted version and the RE's response.

Email

Forward important emails into the project record. An answer that lives only in someone's inbox is lost when that person leaves.

PM notes

If it would matter in a dispute, it goes in the project record the day it happens.

In Procore's own help

Procore Support. Procore's help pages are the reference for how each screen works today.

Common mistakes

  • Notices sent by email and never logged.
  • Meeting action items without owners.
  • Only the latest schedule kept, without the submitted versions.
