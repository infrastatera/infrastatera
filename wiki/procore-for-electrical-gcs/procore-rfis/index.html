RFIs in Procore

Based on Procore's public help pages, November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: in Procore an RFI has an RFI manager who owns it, assignees who answer, and a "ball in court" that always shows who has to act next.

The roles

  • Creator: usually your project engineer or a foreman drafting from the field.
  • RFI manager: the person responsible for the RFI through its life; reviews drafts, assigns them, picks the official response, closes.
  • Assignees: the people asked to answer. While an assignee owes a response, they are ball in court; after they respond, the ball returns to the RFI manager.
  • Distribution list: people kept informed.

Official response

An RFI can collect several responses. The RFI manager marks the one that answers the question as the official response, then closes the RFI or starts a change event if the answer changes the work. See Change events and change orders.

On a Caltrans job

The question goes to the Resident Engineer, who usually answers outside Procore, by letter or email in Caltrans' own format. Practical setup:

  • Keep your RFI log in Procore: number, question, references, dates, status, answer.
  • Send the RFI to the RE in the form the contract or the RE asks for (Procore can produce a PDF).
  • When the answer comes back, record it as the official response and attach the RE's document.
  • Assignees can be internal (your PM reviewing a foreman's question) or a designer if the agency allows direct contact.

What makes a good RFI doesn't change: one question, references, a proposed solution, the impact and the date you need the answer. See Writing RFIs.

Useful habits

  • Create RFIs from a drawing markup so the location is attached.
  • Set a due date that reflects the schedule, not a default.
  • Review the open RFIs list in the weekly meeting by ball in court.

In Procore's own help

Respond to an RFI. Procore's help pages are the reference for how each screen works today.

Common mistakes

  • Several questions in one RFI.
  • Closing an RFI without recording the official answer.
  • An answer that changes cost or time with no change event started.
