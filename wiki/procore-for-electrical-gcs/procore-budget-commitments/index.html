Budget, commitments and subcontracts

Based on Procore's public help pages, November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: the budget shows what each bid item was expected to cost, commitments show what you've agreed to pay subs and suppliers, and together they show where the job is heading before the money is spent.

Budget

  • Built from budget codes in Procore's work breakdown structure (cost code plus cost type such as labor, material, equipment, subcontract).
  • On a Caltrans job, one code per bid item (or group) keeps the budget aligned with the bid and with billing. See Company setup: directory, permissions and cost codes and The bid package: bid items, quantities and the Bid Item List.
  • Loaded by hand or imported from a template.
  • Budget changes move money between lines without changing the contract total.

Commitments

  • Subcontracts and purchase orders, each with its own schedule of values.
  • Subs can be invited to submit invoices against their commitment. See Progress billing and retention.
  • Commitment change orders adjust them. See Change events and change orders.

Direct costs

Costs not tied to a commitment, such as small purchases or your own labor and equipment if they don't come from an integration.

Reading the budget each month

ColumnQuestion it answers
Original budgetWhat did we bid?
Approved changesWhat has the agency added or removed?
CommittedWhat have we agreed to pay others?
Actual / direct costsWhat has been spent?
Forecast to completeWhat will it take to finish?
Projected over/underWhere are we heading?

PM notes

Update the forecast every month. A budget that shows only what was spent tells you about the past; the forecast is what helps you act.

Common mistakes

  • Budget lines that don't match the bid items.
  • Purchase orders placed by phone and never entered as commitments.
  • No forecast, only actuals.
