Procurement and long-lead items

Not yet verified against the current edition

In one sentence: procurement turns approved submittals into material on site at the right time, and long-lead items are where schedules slip.

Typical long-lead items on electrical jobs

  • Controller and equipment cabinets.
  • Poles, mast arms and anchor bolts.
  • Changeable message signs and cameras.
  • Batteries, especially lithium batteries with shipping rules.
  • Network equipment: switches, routers, specific SFP modules.
  • Fiber cable in specific fiber counts.

The cycle

  1. Quote: send suppliers the specification and plan references, not just a part number.
  2. Submit the quoted product. See Submittals: what Caltrans expects.
  3. Purchase order as soon as it is approved, or earlier with the supplier's agreement for very long lead times.
  4. Track fabrication and shipping dates every week.
  5. Receive: check the delivery against the PO and the approved submittal; note damage on the ticket.
  6. Store safely, and keep certificates and manuals for closeout.

Ordering bulk material

For conduit, pull boxes, conductors and fittings, ask the foremen what is left before reordering, and keep a running takeoff against what is installed. See Daily reports and documentation.

PM notes

Put procurement in the schedule with real lead times. A missing cabinet stops a system as surely as a missing crew.

Common mistakes / what Caltrans actually checks

  • Asking for quotes with only a part number.
  • Not tracking fabrication dates after the PO.
  • Accepting a substitute that was never approved.
