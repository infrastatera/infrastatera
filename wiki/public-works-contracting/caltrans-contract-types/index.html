Caltrans contract types: Major, Minor A and Minor B

California law as of November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: Caltrans sorts construction contracts by size into Major, Minor A and Minor B, and the tier decides where the job is advertised, who can bid it as prime and how it is paid.

The three tiers

TierSize (as of November 2026)Advertised onWho can bid as prime
MajorAbove the Minor A limitContractor's CornerAny qualified contractor
Minor AUp to about $1.25 millionContractor's CornerAny qualified contractor
Minor BUnder $461,000Cal eProcureCertified small businesses (limited exceptions)

The limits are not fixed. The Minor B limit follows the lower limit of the State Contract Act and is reviewed every two years, which is why older pages show $333,000 or $388,000. Check the current figure on Caltrans' Contractor's Corner before you rely on it.

Where the money comes from

Minor A and Minor B jobs are funded by the SHOPP Minor Program, the part of the State Highway Operation and Protection Program set aside for small repair, safety and operational projects. The California Transportation Commission approves the program, and Caltrans updates the Minor B project list during the year. Typical electrical Minor B work: detector loop replacement, signal and lighting repairs, conduit and pull box fixes, small CCTV or ramp meter upgrades.

How Minor B is different

  • Small business only. Since 2021, Caltrans has set aside Minor B public works contracts for certified small businesses (SB), with limited exceptions. You need California SB certification to bid as prime. The DVBE goal that applies elsewhere does not apply on SB Minor B contracts.
  • Designed by the district. District Minor B staff prepare the plans, specifications and estimate and administer the contract through completion. The packages are shorter and simpler than a major job's.
  • Advertised by DPAC, not Contractor's Corner. Caltrans' Division of Procurement and Contracts posts Minor B solicitations on Cal eProcure. Search the event name for "Minor B". Plan holder lists are not published on Contractor's Corner for Minor B, so you can't see the competition the same way. Bidder inquiries go through the district (some regions post the questions and answers on their own Caltrans page).
  • Paid in arrears on invoice. There is no monthly progress estimate like on a major contract. You invoice, the work is received, and Caltrans must pay within 45 days of a proper invoice or owe interest. The contract manager can hold money for missing or wrong certified payrolls. See Certified payroll.

Prevailing wage, DIR registration and the other public works rules still apply to Minor B jobs. See Prevailing wage.

Why it matters to a small electrical contractor

Minor B is the easiest way to become a Caltrans prime: small jobs, short schedules, a smaller bond, and you bid only against other small businesses. Winning a few builds the experience and bonding history you need for Minor A and major jobs. See Surety bonding and bonding capacity.

If you look for bids only on Contractor's Corner, you will miss every Minor B job. Add Cal eProcure to the sources in Finding public bids.

PM notes

  • Keep your SB certification current: it can expire, and an expired certification can make your bid nonresponsive.
  • With no progress payments, a Minor B job is financed by you until the invoice is paid. Plan the cash before you bid. See Progress payments and retention.
  • Read the bid documents for the exact limit and rules on each job: they govern over this page.

Common mistakes / what agencies actually check

  • Searching only Contractor's Corner and never seeing Minor B jobs on Cal eProcure.
  • Bidding a Minor B job as prime without a current SB certification.
  • Pricing a Minor B job as if there were monthly progress payments.
  • Using an old Minor B limit from an outdated page.
