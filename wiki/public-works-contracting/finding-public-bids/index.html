Finding public bids

California law as of November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: public bids are advertised in public, but across hundreds of agency sites; the work is in finding the few that fit your license, size, location and crew.

Where agencies advertise

  • Caltrans: its own contract advertisement pages, district by district. See The bid package: bid items, quantities and the Bid Item List for what a Caltrans package contains.
  • Local agencies: their own websites or bid platforms (several cities and counties share commercial platforms), plus legal notices in a newspaper of general circulation.
  • Builders' exchanges and plan rooms: regional services that collect notices and plans.
  • State procurement: the state's eProcurement system, Cal eProcure, for some state departments and for Caltrans Minor B jobs under $461,000. See Caltrans contract types: Major, Minor A and Minor B.
  • Primes looking for subs: if you're a sub, primes advertise for quotes, often with DBE or small business outreach.

Following a bid

Register as a plan holder. It's how you receive addenda, and some agencies only accept bids from registered plan holders. Note the pre-bid meeting (sometimes mandatory), the deadline for questions, and the bid opening date and place.

Go or no-go

Before you spend a week on an estimate, check:

  1. License class required, and the experience requirements if any.
  2. Bond: does it fit your single-job and aggregate limits?
  3. Size and schedule: working days, liquidated damages, night work.
  4. Location: travel and subsistence costs under the prevailing wage determination.
  5. Self-performed share: many contracts require the prime to do a minimum share of the work with its own forces.
  6. Who else is bidding: the plan holders list tells you the competition.

Bid results

Agencies publish bid results after opening. Reading them shows who wins what, at which prices, and where your numbers stand.

Common mistakes / what agencies actually check

  • Missing an addendum because you downloaded the plans without registering as a plan holder.
  • Skipping a mandatory pre-bid meeting.
  • Bidding work far outside the area without pricing travel and subsistence.
