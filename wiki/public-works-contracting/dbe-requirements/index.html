DBE requirements on federal-aid jobs

California law as of November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: on jobs paid with federal transportation money, the DBE program under 49 CFR Part 26 sets rules for using certified disadvantaged businesses, and in late 2026 it is in transition, so read each job's special provisions.

The program

The U.S. Department of Transportation's DBE program applies to contracts funded in part by federal highway, transit and airport money. Caltrans and local agencies spending those funds run it in California. A DBE is a small business certified through the California Unified Certification Program.

What changed in 2025–2026

In October 2025, USDOT issued an Interim Final Rule that substantially revised 49 CFR Part 26, and certified firms are being re-evaluated. Caltrans' local assistance bulletin of August 2026 states that DBE contract goals and goal-based monitoring remain suspended until the re-evaluations are complete and FHWA accepts Caltrans' DBE Program Plan. Other parts of the program, notably prompt payment reporting, remain in force.

What this means for you: on each federal-aid bid, read the special provisions and any addenda for:

  • whether the job has a DBE contract goal and what must be submitted with the bid or after;
  • the forms in use (Caltrans has used a DBE commitment form and a good faith efforts form);
  • the reporting required during the job.

Rules that still matter during the job

  • Prompt payment: pay subs within the time the contract sets (7 days after you're paid on Caltrans and local federal-aid work, under state law) and report monthly when the contract asks. See Paying subcontractors and suppliers on time.
  • Commercially useful function: a DBE counts only for work it actually performs, manages and supervises with its own forces.
  • Terminating or reducing a DBE's committed work needs the agency's written consent and a good-cause reason, with notice to the DBE.

PM notes

Keep the DBE paperwork with the subcontract file, not only in the bid folder: it's the field and payment records that get reviewed during the job.

Common mistakes / what agencies actually check

  • Assuming last year's DBE procedure still applies without reading the current special provisions.
  • Counting a DBE that only passes material through without performing a commercially useful function.
  • Replacing a committed DBE without the agency's written consent.
