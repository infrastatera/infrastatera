The bid package: bid items, quantities and the Bid Item List

Verify the edition named in your contractNot yet verified against the current edition

In one sentence: the bid package is everything you need to price the job: the plans, the Special Provisions, the bid forms and the list of bid items you will be paid on.

What is in it

A Caltrans bid package usually contains:

  • Notice to Bidders and Special Provisions: the bid date, working days, liquidated damages, the DBE goal, and the Special Provisions: what they change and why they win.
  • Project plans. See Project plans: sheet types, legends and electrical sheets.
  • Bid book: the bid forms and certifications, including the Bid Item List.
  • Information handout or supplemental information when available: reports and reference material.
  • Addenda issued before the bid date. Each one must be acknowledged.

The Caltrans Standard Specifications: structure and how to read them and Standard Plans: the ES (electrical) series are part of the contract by reference; they aren't printed in the package.

Bid items

The Bid Item List is the list of things Caltrans pays for, each with an item number, a description, a unit (each, linear foot, lump sum) and an estimated quantity. You enter a unit price for each item.

Two rules matter most:

  • Payment follows the contract, not your cost. The payment clause in the specifications says what each item includes. Work that isn't a separate item is included in other items.
  • Quantities are estimates for unit-price items. You are paid for the quantity actually installed and measured; lump-sum items are paid as a whole.

On electrical jobs, systems are often paid as lump sum (for example, a signal and lighting system at one location), with some items such as conduit or fiber paid by the foot.

What a PM checks before pricing

  1. Read Sections 86 and 87 of the Special Provisions for equipment, testing and payment changes.
  2. Do your own takeoff from the plans and compare it with the Bid Item List.
  3. Mark long-lead equipment and get supplier quotes early. See Procurement and long-lead items.
  4. Check working days, lane closure restrictions and night work: they drive labor cost.
  5. Note anything unclear and ask before the bid date through the bidders' inquiry process.

Common mistakes / what Caltrans actually checks

  • Pricing a lump-sum system without a full takeoff behind it.
  • Assuming work is paid separately when it is included in another item.
  • Missing an addendum.
  • Ignoring lane-closure hours when estimating crew production.
