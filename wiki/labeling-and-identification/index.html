Labeling and identification

Verify the edition named in your contractNot yet verified against the current edition

In one sentence: labels let Caltrans maintenance find and fix things for the next twenty years.

What gets labeled

  • Conductors and cables at both ends and in every pull box.
  • Fibers, splice trays and patch panel ports, matching the splice diagram.
  • Pull box covers with the system marking.
  • Cabinets, breakers and equipment, matching the as-builts.

Good practice

  • Use the label format and material the specifications call for: durable, readable, weather-proof.
  • Label as you pull and terminate, not at the end.
  • Keep labels consistent with the plans, the splice diagram and the as-builts.

Why it matters

Labeling is one of the most frequent findings in Phase 10: Caltrans acceptance testing. Fixing labels after the fact means reopening boxes and cabinets, often with a lane closure.

Common mistakes / what Caltrans actually checks

  • Labeling at the end instead of during installation.
  • Labels that don't match the as-builts.
  • Paper or marker labels that fade.
