Writing RFIs

Not yet verified against the current edition

In one sentence: an RFI is a written question to the engineer when the contract is unclear, contradictory, or doesn't match the field.

When to write one

  • Two documents disagree and the Order of precedence: which contract document wins doesn't settle it.
  • A requirement is unclear or missing.
  • The field doesn't match the plans: a utility in the way, a grade that doesn't work, an existing facility not shown.

What a good RFI contains

  1. A clear subject: location and topic, for example "Pull box at Sta 12+50: conflict with storm drain".
  2. References: sheet numbers, specification sections, photos.
  3. The problem, stated in one or two sentences.
  4. A proposed solution. It speeds the answer and shows you've thought about it.
  5. The impact: what is on hold, and by when you need an answer.

One question per RFI. Number them and keep a log: number, date sent, subject, date answered, answer, and whether it affects cost or time.

After the answer

If the answer changes the work, cost or time, it may lead to a change order. Say so in writing quickly. See Change orders and potholing findings.

PM notes

Send RFIs early. One sent in phase 1 costs a day; one found in the field costs a crew.

Common mistakes / what Caltrans actually checks

  • Several questions in one RFI, so the answer covers only some.
  • No proposed solution.
  • Starting the work before the answer, then not being paid for it.
