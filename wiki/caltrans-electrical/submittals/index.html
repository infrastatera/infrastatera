Submittals: what Caltrans expects

Not yet verified against the current edition

In one sentence: a submittal is the product data, drawings or samples you send for approval before you order or install, and nothing should be installed without it.

Building the log

Go through the Caltrans Standard Specifications: structure and how to read them and the Special Provisions: what they change and why they win and list every "submit": material lists, product data, shop and working drawings, test plans, certificates, schedules. On electrical jobs, Sections 86 and 87 and their Special Provisions produce most of the list.

For each line: specification reference, description, submitted date, response, resubmittal, approval date.

A submittal that gets approved

  • Cover sheet with the contract number, the specification section and the item.
  • Highlight the exact model, options and ratings on the cut sheet; cross out what doesn't apply.
  • Show compliance: point to where each requirement is met.
  • One subject per submittal, but complete.
  • Include the Caltrans form or format the contract or the Resident Engineer requires.

Review time

The specifications set how long Caltrans has to review. Build that time, and a possible resubmittal, into the schedule. See Procurement and long-lead items.

PM notes

Submit long-lead items first, and submit them complete: a returned submittal restarts the clock.

Common mistakes / what Caltrans actually checks

  • Ordering material before it is approved.
  • Cut sheets without the actual model highlighted.
  • Submittals that leave out a requirement from the Special Provisions.
