Magnetic particle and dye penetrant testing

Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: both methods make a crack you can barely see visible: magnetic particles gather where a crack disturbs a magnetic field, and penetrant dye bleeds out of a crack onto a white developer.

Magnetic particle testing (MT)

  1. Clean the surface (coatings thicker than a little reduce sensitivity).
  2. Magnetize the area with a yoke (an electromagnet with two legs) placed across the expected crack direction.
  3. Apply fine iron particles, dry powder or a liquid suspension, often visible or fluorescent.
  4. Particles gather at leakage fields where a crack interrupts the magnetic flux, showing its line.
  5. Repeat with the yoke turned about 90 degrees: MT finds cracks best when they run across the field.

Works on ferromagnetic steel only, finds surface and slightly subsurface cracks. Standardized in ASTM E709 and E1444.

Dye penetrant testing (PT)

  1. Clean and dry the surface thoroughly.
  2. Apply a red or fluorescent penetrant and wait the dwell time (often 5 to 30 minutes).
  3. Remove the excess from the surface.
  4. Apply a thin white developer; penetrant trapped in cracks bleeds out and shows as red lines or glows under UV light.

Works on any non-porous material (steel, stainless, aluminum, welds), finds only surface-breaking defects. Standardized in ASTM E165.

Uses on structures

  • Confirming and measuring suspected fatigue cracks at welded details. See Steel defects: corrosion, fatigue cracks and connections.
  • Checking weld toes and repairs.
  • Inspecting after a crack has been arrested with drilled holes, to see whether the crack tip was caught.

Limits

Both need clean surfaces and good access. Neither tells you how deep a crack goes: use Ultrasonic testing of steel and welds for that.

Common mistakes

  • Testing through paint and declaring no cracks.
  • Using one yoke orientation only.
  • Cutting the penetrant dwell time short.
