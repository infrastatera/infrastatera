Glossary

ACDEFGHILMNPRSTU
AASHTOAmerican Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials
The organization whose manuals, including the Manual for Bridge Element Inspection, set bridge practice in the U.S.
ASRAlkali-silica reaction
A reaction between alkalis in cement and some silica in aggregates, forming a gel that swells and cracks concrete, often in a map pattern. Read more
Carbonation
The reaction of carbon dioxide with concrete that lowers its alkalinity from the surface inward; when it reaches the steel, corrosion can start. Read more
Chain drag
Dragging chains across a deck to hear hollow-sounding delaminated areas. Read more
Condition state
One of four levels (good, fair, poor, severe) used to record quantities of a bridge element in element-level inspection. Read more
Couplant
A gel or liquid placed between an ultrasonic probe and the surface so sound passes into the material. Read more
Cover
The thickness of concrete between the surface and the nearest reinforcing bar. Read more
Delamination
A hidden plane of separation in concrete, usually at the level of the reinforcement and caused by corrosion; it sounds hollow when struck. Read more
Efflorescence
White mineral deposits left on concrete or masonry by water passing through it. Read more
Element-level inspection
Inspection that records the quantity of each bridge element in each condition state. Read more
Fatigue crack
A crack that grows under repeated loading, typically starting at welded details and other stress concentrations in steel. Read more
GPRGround-penetrating radar
A method that sends radio pulses into a material and records reflections from bars, ducts, voids and layers. Read more
Half-cell potential
The electrical potential of reinforcement measured against a reference electrode, used to estimate the probability of active corrosion. Read more
Impact-echo
A method that strikes the surface and reads the frequency of reflected stress waves to find thickness and flaws. Read more
Load rating
The calculated live load a bridge can safely carry, used to decide whether it must be posted for lower weights. Read more
MTMagnetic particle testing
A surface method that shows cracks in ferromagnetic steel by the gathering of magnetic particles at leakage fields. Read more
NBINational Bridge Inventory
The federal database of bridges on public roads and their inspection data. Read more
NBISNational Bridge Inspection Standards
The federal regulation (23 CFR 650, Subpart C) setting how highway bridges are inspected and reported. Read more
NDTNon-destructive testing
Testing that examines a material or structure without damaging it; also called NDE (evaluation). Read more
NSTMNonredundant steel tension member
A steel member in tension whose failure could cause the bridge to collapse; formerly called a fracture-critical member. Read more
PAUTPhased array ultrasonic testing
Ultrasonic testing with many elements fired in sequence to steer the beam and image a cross-section. Read more
PTPenetrant testing
A surface method that shows cracks by drawing a dye out of them with a developer. Read more
Rebound number
The reading of a rebound hammer, related to surface hardness of concrete. Read more
RTRadiographic testing
Imaging the inside of a part with X-rays or gamma rays. Read more
Scour
Erosion of soil around bridge foundations by flowing water. Read more
Section loss
Loss of steel thickness from corrosion, measured as remaining thickness or percentage lost. Read more
SHMStructural health monitoring
Sensors and systems that measure a structure's behavior and condition over time. Read more
SNBISpecifications for the National Bridge Inventory
The 2022 data specifications for reporting bridge inventory and inspection data, replacing the old coding guide. Read more
Spall
A piece of concrete that has broken away from the surface, often over corroded reinforcement. Read more
Team leader
The qualified, certified person in charge of a bridge inspection team under the NBIS. Read more
UPVUltrasonic pulse velocity
The speed of an ultrasonic pulse through concrete, used to judge uniformity and find internal flaws. Read more
UTUltrasonic testing
Testing with high-frequency sound to measure thickness or find internal flaws, mainly in steel and welds. Read more

For learning only, not engineering advice. Inspections, test methods and their interpretation belong to qualified, certified people working to the standards and the owner's procedures that apply.
