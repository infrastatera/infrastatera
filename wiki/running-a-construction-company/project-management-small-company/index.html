Project management basics for a small company

California, November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: in a small company the owner, the PM and the foreman share project management, so write down who owns each task and keep a short weekly routine.

Who owns what

TaskUsually owned by
Contract, insurance, bondsOwner / office
Job start meeting, budget, schedulePM, with the foreman
Submittals, RFIs, ordersPM
Daily work, crew, safety, daily reportsForeman
Hours and cost codesForeman, checked by the PM
Change orders and noticesPM, with the foreman's facts
BillingPM and office
CloseoutPM, foreman and office

The job start meeting

Before work starts, the PM and foreman review scope, estimate (hours and quantities by cost code), schedule, long-lead material, risks, safety plan and who the owner's contacts are.

The weekly routine

  • Monday: two-week look-ahead with each foreman: work, crew, material, equipment, inspections.
  • Midweek: submittals, RFIs and orders reviewed; open items chased.
  • Friday: hours checked against cost codes; changes and problems logged; next week planned.
  • Every two weeks: job cost review. See Job costing: knowing what each job really made.
  • Monthly: billing, cash forecast, WIP update.

Tools

A shared job folder, a submittal and RFI log, a change log and a two-week schedule are enough to start. Field apps help once the routine exists. See The day-to-day of a GC project manager.

PM notes

Most job problems are visible two weeks ahead in the look-ahead. The routine's job is to look.

Common mistakes

  • No job start meeting, so the foreman never sees the estimate.
  • The owner doing everything and the PM waiting for decisions.
  • Change orders handled from memory.
