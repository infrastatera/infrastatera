From sub to prime

California, November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: as a prime you hold the owner's contract, the bonds and the coordination of every sub, so step up on jobs where your own trade is most of the work.

What changes

TopicAs a subAs a prime
ContractWith a GCWith the owner or agency
BondsSometimesBid, performance and payment bonds, usually
CoordinationYour scopeAll trades, traffic control, testing, schedule
PaperworkYour submittals and payrollEveryone's: submittals, RFIs, certified payroll, DBE, closeout
PaymentFrom the GCFrom the owner; you pay the subs
RiskYour scopeThe whole job, including your subs' failures

Where specialty primes win

Agencies often advertise work where one trade is most of the scope: on Caltrans, electrical jobs like lighting, signals, ramp metering and communications are bid with an electrical contractor as prime. See What a Caltrans electrical contractor does.

Making the step gradually

  1. Start with small jobs within your trade where the other trades are minor.
  2. Build relationships with reliable subs (traffic control, paving, striping, civil).
  3. Line up bonding before you bid. See Bonding capacity: how it is set and how to grow it.
  4. Add the office capacity for prime paperwork: submittals, RFIs, certified payroll, DBE, subcontractor listing. See Listing subcontractors and DBE requirements on federal-aid jobs.
  5. Learn from each job's closeout before bidding bigger.

PM notes

Your subs' performance becomes yours. Choose them on reliability, insurance and payroll compliance, not just price.

Common mistakes

  • Bidding as prime without bonding lined up.
  • Underpricing the coordination and paperwork.
  • Choosing subs on lowest price alone.
