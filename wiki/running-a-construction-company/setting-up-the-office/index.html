Setting up the office

California, November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: a contractor's office is a short list of systems (bank, books, payroll, insurance, files and a renewals calendar) that must work every week, whoever is on vacation.

The minimum set

SystemWhat good looks like
BankBusiness accounts only, separate from personal; a separate account for payroll is common
BookkeepingConstruction-aware accounting software with jobs and cost codes from day one
PayrollA provider that handles California rules, certified payroll if you do public works, and workers' comp reporting
InsuranceWorkers' comp, general liability, auto, equipment; a broker who knows contractors
FilesOne shared, backed-up folder structure: company, jobs, people, equipment
Renewals calendarLicense, bonds, insurance, DIR registration, vehicle registrations, certifications

Workers' compensation

Any contractor with employees must carry workers' compensation insurance. California law also moves toward requiring it for every licensed contractor, with or without employees; the date has been pushed back before, so check CSLB for the current requirement. See Insurance requirements on public contracts for the other policies.

Files that outlive people

Set up the job folder template once (contract, insurance, submittals, RFIs, change orders, daily reports, photos, billing, closeout) and use it on every job. When someone leaves, the job's history stays.

Renewals calendar

Put every expiry date in one shared calendar with a reminder 60 days before. A lapsed bond, a lapsed workers' comp policy or a lapsed DIR registration can stop work or a bid.

Software, step by step

Start with accounting and payroll. Add a field app (daily reports, photos, time) when the paperwork from the field starts slipping. See Is Procore worth it for a small electrical GC? for how to judge a larger platform.

Official sources

CSLB: workers' compensation for licensees (B&P 7125).

Common mistakes

  • Mixing personal and business money.
  • Accounting software set up without jobs and cost codes.
  • Renewal dates kept in one person's head.
