Choosing your market

California, November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: a small contractor makes money by being very good at a narrow kind of work for a known set of customers, so choose your market on purpose instead of bidding everything.

Public or private

Public worksPrivate work
Finding workPublished bids, open to all qualified biddersRelationships and invitations
AwardUsually lowest responsive, responsible bidPrice, relationship, reputation
PaperworkHeavy: prevailing wage, certified payroll, bonds, submittalsLighter, depends on the owner
PaymentSlow but sureVaries with the owner's finances

See What makes a job public works in California.

Prime or sub

  • As a sub, you deal with one GC, a narrower scope and less paperwork, but the GC controls schedule and payment.
  • As a prime, you deal with the owner directly and control the job, but carry bonding, coordination and all the risk. See From sub to prime.

Narrowing down

  1. Your strength: which work do you do better and cheaper than others? (Use job cost history, not feelings.)
  2. Customers: which agencies, GCs or owners pay well and treat subs fairly?
  3. Geography: how far can crews travel and stay profitable?
  4. Job size: the range your bonding, cash and crews can carry.
  5. Competition: how many bidders show up, and how close are the results?

Using bid results

Public bid results show who bids, how often and how far apart. Track them for your target agencies to see where you're competitive. See Finding public bids.

PM notes

Write your target market in one sentence and check every bid against it. "Caltrans and city electrical work under $3 million within 60 miles" is a market; "anything electrical" isn't.

Common mistakes

  • Bidding every job you hear about.
  • Choosing a market by revenue instead of profit.
  • Ignoring how a customer pays until the first job.
