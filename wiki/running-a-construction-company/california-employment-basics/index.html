Employment basics in California

California, November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: California has stricter wage rules than federal law, with daily overtime, timed meal and rest breaks and strict classification of workers, and construction is one of the most audited industries.

Employee or independent contractor

California uses the ABC test to decide whether a worker is an employee. In construction, a subcontractor relationship can escape the ABC test only under specific conditions in Labor Code section 2781, including a written subcontract and a subcontractor that holds its own CSLB license and runs its own business. A worker you pay by the job without those conditions is very likely your employee. See the DLSE's independent contractor FAQ.

Overtime

For construction (IWC Wage Order 16), non-exempt workers generally earn:

  • 1.5× the regular rate after 8 hours in a day or 40 in a week;
  • 2× after 12 hours in a day, with special rules for the seventh consecutive day.

Prevailing wage jobs have their own overtime rates in the wage determination. See Prevailing wage.

Meal and rest breaks

  • A paid 10-minute rest break for every 4 hours worked (or major fraction).
  • An unpaid 30-minute meal break when working more than 5 hours, starting no later than the end of the fifth hour; a second one after 10 hours. The first can be waived by mutual consent only if the day is 6 hours or less.
  • A missed break generally costs one hour of pay at the regular rate, per worker, per day.

Pay records

Itemized wage statements with the required information, accurate time records and payroll records kept for the required period. Give new hires the written wage notice required by Labor Code section 2810.5.

Heat

Cal/OSHA's heat illness prevention rules apply to outdoor work: water, shade, rest, training and a written plan.

PM notes

Track time and breaks daily, in the field, not rebuilt at the end of the week. Most wage claims are about time records that can't be proved. Talk to an employment attorney before setting up pay practices.

Official sources

DIR: construction employer brochure · DLSE: independent contractor vs. employee.

Common mistakes

  • Paying workers as "subs" without the conditions of Labor Code 2781.
  • Weekly overtime only, ignoring daily overtime.
  • Lunch at hour six on a ten-hour day.
