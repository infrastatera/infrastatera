Saying no: which jobs to pass on

California, November 2026Last verified November 25, 2026

In one sentence: every bid costs estimating time and every job uses crews, cash and bonding, so decide on purpose which ones to pursue, and walk away from the rest.

A bid/no-bid checklist

Score each from 1 to 5:

QuestionWhat to check
FitIs this the work and customer type you target? See Choosing your market
CapacityCrews, foremen and equipment available when it runs?
Bonding and cashWithin limits? What does the first 90 days of cash look like?
Owner / GCHow do they pay and treat contractors?
RiskUnusual terms, liquidated damages, schedule, site conditions, design quality
CompetitionHow many bidders usually, and how close?
MarginRealistic margin after overhead?

Set a minimum score; below it, don't bid.

Red flags

  • Contract terms that shift design risk or unlimited damages to you.
  • A schedule that can't be met with your crews.
  • An owner or GC known for slow payment or disputes.
  • A scope far outside your experience.
  • Bidding only to keep the crew busy at a loss.

Saying no well

Thank the GC or owner, say you're not able to bid this one, and ask to stay on their list. A clear no is better than a weak bid.

PM notes

Track your hit rate by customer and job type. If you win 1 in 20 with a GC, your estimating time is better spent elsewhere.

Common mistakes

  • Bidding every invitation.
  • Reading the contract terms only after winning.
  • Taking a job you can't staff because the price was right.
