Traffic signals

Verify the edition named in your contractNot yet verified against the current edition

In one sentence: a traffic signal is a controller in a cabinet that switches signal heads in a timed sequence, adjusted by what the detectors see.

Main parts

PartWhat it does
Controller and cabinetRuns the timing; Caltrans uses standard controller and cabinet models named in the contract. See Equipment cabinets
Signal standards and mast armsHold the heads over and beside the road. See Foundations: cabinet, pole and sign
Signal headsVehicle and pedestrian indications, usually LED
Pedestrian push buttonsCall a pedestrian phase; accessible push buttons where required
DetectionTells the controller where vehicles are. See Vehicle detection
Emergency vehicle preemptionGives emergency vehicles a green, when specified
Battery backupKeeps the signal running in a power outage. See Battery backup systems
Signal and interconnect cableFrom cabinet to heads, and to other signals or the TMC

Details are on the ES Standard Plans: the ES (electrical) series and the signal sheets of the plans.

How it is built

Conduit and pull boxes, foundations, standards and cabinet, cable, heads and buttons, then cabinet wiring and the turn-on.

Turn-on

A new or modified signal is turned on in a planned sequence agreed with Caltrans: timing loaded, heads checked, often with temporary traffic control and Caltrans staff present.

PM notes

  • Signal work at an existing intersection is often staged to keep the signal running. Plan temporary signals or staging.
  • Order the controller cabinet early; it may need factory testing.

Common mistakes / what Caltrans actually checks

  • Turning on without the agreed timing loaded.
  • Heads aimed wrong.
  • Push buttons not meeting accessibility requirements.
